## From `curaition/daily-publishing-prompt` (canonical as of 16 Aug 2026)

### Pre-production

- Read the tone-of-voice guide and newsletter example TOVs before producing
  any output.
- Run click-bait-scout and cultural-scout in parallel. Present both
  candidates for editorial sign-off.
- Before presenting candidates: `curaition_get_stats` canary check
  (`effective_org_id: null`, `external_safe: true`); `curaition_list_content`
  by published_at desc, limit 20; the full domain sweep is mandatory, not
  optional; verify all specific factual claims via web search before writing
  anything.

### Standing rules

- Issue count: update each session.
- Slide 1 = hook only. 1-2 lines. Specific fact + reframe/withhold.
- All content slides: 66px / 1.31 line-height. Max 3 lines, ideally 2. Text
  centred. Colour: olive #6B7A3F throughout.
- LinkedIn post opens with the Substack subtitle as the first line, then
  mirrors the Substack opening throughout.
- No em dashes. No double hyphens. Anywhere.
- Write everything to read as discovered, not constructed: uneven rhythm, no
  parallel short-sentence structures.

### On approval

Answer first: what does this add up to? What does the reader leave with that
they didn't arrive with? Apply the Veritasium engagement formula:
misconception → question→explanation → A plot + B plot. Titles challenge a
belief and open a gap simultaneously. The data chart slide is the moment the
question becomes concrete in numbers.

### Outputs

- **Substack article** — 1,000 words max. The Drop. No sources block at the
  bottom; sources inline only. Title creates a tension or reversal, never
  resolves the argument. Every issue requires a subtitle: title creates the
  gap, subtitle hints at the answer without resolving it.
- **LinkedIn post** — opens with the Substack subtitle as the first line,
  mirrors the Substack opening object and register. Same dry voice,
  compressed not summarised. 150-250 words. Ends "Full breakdown in the
  comments". Max 3 hashtags: #CulturalIntelligence #BrandStrategy + one
  variable.
- **IG carousel** — 8 content slides + 1 final slide, 1080x1350 PNG (4:5).
  Instagram's API accepts aspect ratios from 4:5 to 1.91:1 only; the former
  1080x1440 (3:4) canvas was below that floor and could not be posted via API.
- **LinkedIn carousel PDF** — same slides, 1080x1350 PNG per slide, compiled
  to PDF. Upload via the document icon, not the image icon.
- **IG caption** — one line, declarative, ends with relevant emoji.
- **First comment** — Substack link + hashtags: #culturalintelligence
  #brandstrategy + 2-3 topic-specific + #curaition.

### Carousel technical spec

- Watermark: the curAItion logo as olive PNG (dark pixels → #6B7A3F, light →
  transparent), 32x32px, horizontally centred, bottom at 96.9% of slide
  height. No watermark on the final slide.
- Chart slide: vertical bars only, SVG rect elements. Baseline at 86.5% of
  slide height; tallest bar 396px. Title left-aligned at y=110. Axis
  baseline 1px #C8C3B4. Bar colour sage #9CAF7A. Generous empty space above
  the bars.
- Final slide: mycelium mark 40px + wordmark "curAItion" Geist Medium 42px,
  both entirely olive (the AI is NOT contrasted). CTA "Read the full story
  at curaition.substack.com", Geist Medium 26px, bottom-centre.
- LinkedIn final slide: same lockup + "Follow curAItion for daily cultural
  intelligence" Geist Light 300 + CTA at the bottom.
- Slide numbers: top-right, Geist Light 300, 28px, #C8C3B4, format N/9.
- Fonts: Geist Medium 500 = content text; Geist Light 300 = slide numbers.
  Embed woff2 as base64 data URIs, never CDN.
- Background: #F1EFE8 cream throughout. Text centred, olive, never dark ink
  on content slides.
- Widow gate: merge last line if 6 chars or fewer.

## From `curaition/carousel-slide-density` (15 Aug 2026, reach-data-backed)

**Maximum 3 lines per slide. Ideally 2. The best slides are 1-2 lines.** The
gap between two lines is where the interest lives. The hook is the most
stripped-back slide in the deck.

Canonical reach-ranked hooks (benchmark every slide 1 against these):

- "England are in the World Cup semifinal. / They left their best right back
  at home." (331 reach — fact + reframe, nothing explained)
- "Andy Burnham went to Cambridge. / Cambridge radicalised him. / On Monday
  he's Prime Minister." (297 — three lines, each contradicting the last)
- "Vogue published an article this week asking who decides good taste now. /
  That's the signal." (296 — observation + verdict)
- "Jannik Sinner just won Wimbledon. / Again." (289 — the pause is
  everything)
- "Yesterday, Ariana Grande's rep issued a statement. / One word in it is
  doing all the work." (259 — withholds the punchline, forces the swipe)
- "China's Gen Z stopped buying status." (237 — the statement IS the
  argument)
- "France had Mbappé. Dembélé. Olise. / Spain had a system." (229 — list
  then reversal)
- "BTS didn't just play London." (215 — everything withheld)

The pattern: short first line stating a specific fact or naming the specific
object; second line that reframes, contradicts, withholds, or delivers a
flat verdict; no third line unless each line does something the previous one
doesn't. What kills it: explaining the observation after making it; a third
line that summarises; parallel sentence structures; starting with a
question.

## From `people/ben-oliver-voice` (essentials)

Used for repost comments only, not the assets. Dry, specific, peer-to-peer;
wit in the precision, not the punchline. Opens on the specific thing, never
a compliment. Brooker rhythm: short, short, short, then the longer sentence
with the real point. Never explains the joke; never cheerleads. British
English; no em dashes; no double hyphens. On LinkedIn reposts, minimum three
emojis, used where they add. "Anyway" as a closer carries more weight than
any punchline.
