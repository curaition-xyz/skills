Bitcoin went up while tech went down. That's the headline. It's the least interesting thing that happened.

On 2 July, Bitcoin held above $61,000 and Ethereum added around 5%, while the Nasdaq fell. For years the standard line on crypto was that it's leveraged Nasdaq. A risk asset that moves with the tech tape it claims to replace. For one session, it didn't.

The trigger wasn't crypto. It was Kevin Warsh, at the ECB forum in Sintra, talking about productivity and the room it might give the Fed to cut. A soft June jobs print, 57,000, did the rest. A man who no longer sets policy moved the market more than the people who do.

Here's the part the day-one coverage skips. The decoupling thesis isn't new. We've been tracking it in the CurAItion library since March, when the argument was that the macro backdrop had finally handed Bitcoin its chance to break correlation. The argument sat there for a quarter. On 2 July the price agreed with it.

So is it real. The flows say no. Spot Bitcoin ETFs bled around $296 million on the day, most of it from IBIT. A rally the institutions are selling into is a narrative, not a trend.

Price is what you see. Flows are what you fund. Which one are you trading?
