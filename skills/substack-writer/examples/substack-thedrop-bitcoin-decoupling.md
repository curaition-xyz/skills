# The Decoupling Is Real. The Buyers Aren't.

On 2 July, Bitcoin held above $61,000, up around 2.7% on the day. Ethereum added roughly 5%, to about $1,696. Solana ran 22% on the week after launching an on-chain governance mechanism. None of that is the story. The story is what the rest of the market did at the same time: the Nasdaq fell around 0.8%, a day after the S&P had closed lower.

For years the standard dismissal of crypto has been that it is leveraged Nasdaq. A risk asset that rises and falls with the tech tape it claims to be an alternative to. For one session, that stopped being true.

## The catalyst doesn't work at the Fed anymore

The trigger was macro, not crypto. Kevin Warsh, speaking at the ECB forum in Sintra, pointed to AI-driven productivity gains and the room they might give the Fed to cut rates. A soft June payrolls print, 57,000 jobs, did the rest. Rate-cut hopes returned, and capital moved towards risk.

Sit with the oddity. The named catalyst is a man who no longer sets policy, reacting to a jobs number weak enough to be read as good news.

## We've seen this argument before

Here is the part a day-one write-up misses. The decoupling thesis is not new. We have been tracking it in the CurAItion library since March, when Bitcoin Magazine Pro argued that the macro backdrop had finally handed Bitcoin its best chance to break correlation with equities.

The argument sat there for a quarter. On 2 July, the price agreed with it. That is worth noting, and worth suspicion. A thesis that has been about to happen for three months has a habit of not happening.

## The flows tell a different story

So is this the real decoupling. The honest answer is in the flows, and the flows say not yet.

Even as prices rose, spot Bitcoin ETFs kept bleeding. Around $296 million left on the day, roughly $220 million of it from IBIT alone. A rally that the institutional vehicles are selling into is a retail and technical move. It is not a mandate. The most patient money in the market is not validating this.

That is the tension worth holding. Price is what you can see. Flows are what people are willing to fund. On 2 July, the two disagreed.

## One thing worth watching

Two conditions would turn this from a story into a signal. The decoupling surviving the next real equity down-day. And ETF flows turning positive. Until both happen, treat the break as unconfirmed.

In the meantime, the more interesting number is not the price. It is the $296 million that walked out of the ETFs while the price went up.
