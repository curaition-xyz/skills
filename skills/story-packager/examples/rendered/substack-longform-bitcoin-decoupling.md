# The Decoupling Is Real. The Buyers Aren't.

*Bitcoin held above $61,000 as equities slid — the break crypto has waited years for. Whether it holds is the only question that matters, and the ETF flows are the tell.*

For years the bear's favourite jibe about crypto was that it's just leveraged Nasdaq — a risk asset cosplaying as a revolution, rising and falling with the same tech stocks it claims to replace. On 2 July, for one day at least, that stopped being true.

Bitcoin climbed roughly 2% and held above $61,000, with Ethereum up about 5% — while the [Nasdaq fell around 0.8%](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/07/02/crypto-market-today-july-2-bitcoin-breaks-away-from-tech-stocks-to-surge-above-usd61-000/), the day after the S&P had closed lower. The green candle isn't the interesting part. The divergence is.

## What actually moved

Bitcoin above $61K; Ether up around 5% to roughly $1,696; and Solana the week's standout, [up about 22%](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/07/02/crypto-market-today-july-2-bitcoin-breaks-away-from-tech-stocks-to-surge-above-usd61-000/) after launching a new on-chain governance mechanism. Equities, meanwhile, went the other way. On a single day, that's noise. As a pattern, it's the thing crypto has promised and rarely delivered: price action that doesn't just track the tech tape.

## Why now

The proximate trigger was macro, not crypto-native. Ex-Fed governor Kevin Warsh, speaking at the [ECB Forum in Sintra](https://coinpedia.org/news/why-is-crypto-market-going-up-today-july-2nd/), pointed to AI-driven productivity gains and room for the Fed to eventually cut rates — and a soft June payrolls print (+57,000 jobs) did the rest, reviving rate-cut hopes and sending capital toward risk. Note the oddity worth sitting with: the named catalyst is a man who no longer sets policy, reacting to a jobs number weak enough to be read as good news.

## We've heard this before

Here's the part a pure-news write-up misses. The decoupling thesis isn't new. It's been [tracked since March](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0QNsBVobp2U), when Bitcoin Magazine Pro argued the macro backdrop gave Bitcoin its best chance yet to break correlation with equities. The argument has been sitting there for a quarter. What changed on 2 July is that the price finally agreed with it — which is worth something, and also worth suspicion, because a thesis that has been *about to happen* for three months has a way of un-happening.

## The tell

So is this the real decoupling? The honest answer is in the flows, and the flows say *not yet*. Even as prices rose, spot Bitcoin ETFs kept bleeding — roughly [$296 million in outflows, about $220 million from IBIT alone](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/07/02/crypto-market-today-july-2-bitcoin-breaks-away-from-tech-stocks-to-surge-above-usd61-000/). A rally the institutional vehicles are selling into is a retail-and-technical move, not a mandate.

> A rally the ETFs are selling into is a narrative, not yet a trend.

## So what

If you hold Bitcoin, the takeaway isn't "the decoupling is here." It's: treat it as unconfirmed. A one-day break, driven by a non-voting ex-governor's remarks, with institutional money still walking out the door, is a narrative peg — not a trend change. Two things would turn it from story into signal: the decoupling surviving the next real equity down-day, and ETF flows flipping positive. Until then, the more interesting number isn't the price. It's the $296 million that left while the price went up.
