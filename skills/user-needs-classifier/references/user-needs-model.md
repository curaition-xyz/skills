# The User Needs Model 2.0

Source: *"The Evolution of Audience-Driven Publishing: User Needs Model 2.0"*,
smartocto in collaboration with Dmitry Shishkin (March 2023). Shishkin led
the original 2017 BBC News "user needs" research (internally nicknamed
"Dima's wheel of news") that this model extends. Adopted in some form by
BBC News, Wall Street Journal, Vogue, BuzzFeed, The Atlantic, Vox, LAist,
South China Morning Post, DPG Media, Mediahuis, and others.

## The core idea

Readers don't come to a piece of content for "news" in the abstract — they
come with one of a small number of underlying needs. A newsroom (or a
cultural-intelligence studio) that's deliberate about which need a given
piece serves gets better engagement than one that produces everything as a
flat factual update. The model gives a shared vocabulary for that.

## The four axes (drivers)

Every need sits on exactly one of four axes, each answering a different
question about what the reader wants:

| Axis | Question | Drive |
|---|---|---|
| **Know** | Know something | fact-driven |
| **Understand** | Understand something | context-driven |
| **Feel** | Feel something | emotion-driven |
| **Do** | Do something | action-driven |

## The eight needs

Two needs per axis. This is the 2.0 model's structure: the original BBC
model was thinner on the "Do" axis, so two needs were added there (Help me,
Connect me), and one need was reframed for clarity (Keep me on trend →
Keep me engaged).

### Know (fact-driven)

**Update me** — the core fact-driven need. What happened, when, where. The
classic breaking-news piece: a hurricane hit, an earnings report dropped, a
law passed. This is the easiest need to default to and, per smartocto's
data, the one most newsrooms overproduce relative to how much it's actually
read (see §Data insight below).

**Keep me engaged** *(reframed from "Keep me on trend" in 2.0)* — staying
current with an ongoing conversation and participating in it, not just
passively knowing a fact happened. The 2.0 authors broadened this
deliberately: the original "Keep me on trend" felt too narrowly tied to
social-media virality. Keep me engaged is about the broader current of
what's being discussed — round-ups of reactions, "what people are saying",
commentary on how a story is being covered elsewhere.

### Understand (context-driven)

**Give me perspective** — analysis and interpretation: what does this
*mean*, is it part of a bigger pattern, should the reader be more or less
worried than the raw facts suggest. This is the "so what" layer on top of a
fact.

**Educate me** — explains something the reader doesn't already understand:
a mechanism, a system, a piece of background knowledge needed to make sense
of the topic at all. The difference from Give me perspective: Educate me
teaches a concept: Give me perspective interprets a situation. ("How do
dykes work" is Educate me; "should you be worried about a new flood" is
Give me perspective.)

### Feel (emotion-driven)

**Inspire me** — uplift, motivation, hope. Personal stories of people who
overcame something, positive-news framing, "good news" antidotes to doom
framing.

**Divert me** — entertainment, distraction, escape. Doesn't have to teach
or move the reader forward — just needs to be engaging or diverting in the
moment (a visual essay, a slideshow, something purely absorbing).

### Do (action-driven)

**Help me** *(new in 2.0)* — practical, personal-level utility: "news you
can use." Solutions journalism, how-to guidance, checklists, clear steps
the reader can take for themselves. This fills the gap left by the service
sector's self-sufficiency content — media doing "personal guidance" rather
than just self-serve information.

**Connect me** *(new in 2.0)* — societal, not personal: content that
connects people around a shared idea, cause, or place, building belonging
and community pride. Particularly important for regional/local publishers.
The distinction from Help me: Help me solves *your* problem; Connect me
makes you feel part of something bigger than yourself.

## Nuances worth remembering

- **Update me sits near the Understand axis in the model's visual layout**
  even though it's fact-driven, because pure updates often blur into
  context ("a hurricane hit Haiti" edges toward "hurricanes are getting
  stronger due to climate change" — that's already interpretation, not
  pure fact).
- **Connect me and Inspire me are drawn next to each other** in the model
  despite sitting on different axes (do vs. feel) — the pull to feel
  connected to a cause borders on the pull to feel personally motivated,
  even though one is societal/collective and the other personal/emotional.
- **Brand-specific needs are welcome as a further ring around the eight.**
  Publishers analysed in the source paper layered their own named needs on
  top — e.g. Vox's "Connect micro to macro", the Atlantic's "Let me take a
  meaningful brake", New York Times's "Enrich my life", Wall Street
  Journal's "Give me an edge", TBIJ's "Show me how the world is affected by
  it". All of these still map cleanly onto one of the eight core needs —
  they're flavour, not a ninth category. Feel free to name a
  candidate-specific flavour in the rationale if it sharpens the framing,
  but always classify against the core eight first.

## The format-vs-need pitfall

The single most common mistake newsrooms make applying this model: confusing
a **format** (the shape an editor chooses — analysis, explainer, listicle,
video, timeline) with a **need** (the psychological job the piece does for
the reader). An analysis piece *can* serve Give me perspective, but editors
often reflexively assume "analysis format = Give me perspective need" and
stop thinking about it — when in fact the same analysis format could be
serving Educate me if what it's really doing is teaching a concept. Always
ask what job the piece does for the reader, independent of its shape.

## Data insight: why balance matters

Across the brands smartocto studied, Update me pieces were consistently the
most overproduced and among the worst-performing per piece: in one analysed
brand, ~57% of published articles were Update me but they earned only 8.5%
of article reads. Give me perspective pieces tended to be well-balanced
(production roughly matches reads). Educate me pieces were consistently
under-produced relative to how well they performed — "there's clearly scope
to produce more of this type." Help me pieces, where produced, often had the
best per-piece read performance despite being one of the least-produced
types. The practical takeaway: a portfolio skewed toward Update me is a
warning sign, not a neutral default.

## Question bank (generalise these, don't copy verbatim)

Use these as the *shape* of the questions to ask about a specific
candidate — always rewrite them against the actual facts, never hand back
the generic version.

**Know / Update me**: What specific events or developments will happen
next, and when? Can this be laid out as a timeline or plain factual piece
of what/when/where?

**Know / Keep me engaged**: How is this being discussed or reacted to right
now — social commentary, notable quotes, how other outlets are covering it?
Is there a documentary, retrospective, or other coverage angle already
circulating?

**Understand / Educate me**: What's the underlying mechanism, system, or
piece of background knowledge a reader needs to make sense of this at all?
Can it be explained step by step?

**Understand / Give me perspective**: Is there reason to think this will
recur, escalate, or matter more/less than it appears? What do
experts/comparable cases/data say about whether concern is warranted?

**Feel / Inspire me**: Is there a person at the center of this who can speak
to how it changed them, or who overcame something? What's the personal,
motivating angle?

**Feel / Divert me**: Is there strong visual material — footage, images, a
slideshow — that can carry the story on its own, without requiring
analysis?

**Do / Help me**: Is there something concrete a reader can do differently
because of this, on a personal level? Can this become a checklist, guide,
or set of practical steps?

**Do / Connect me**: Is there a movement, community, or shared identity this
story could rally people around? Is there a way to invite the reader to
participate, not just watch?
